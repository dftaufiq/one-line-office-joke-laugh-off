# Variants — One-Line Office Joke Laugh-Off

Champion selection by internal pairwise judging, mirroring the real judge:
criteria applied lexicographically per RULES.txt — (1) is a one-line
office joke, (2) sharp surprising punchline / real laugh, (3) clear on
one read, (4) fresh / avoids clichés, (5) tight wording — AND margin:
pick the variant that wins DECISIVELY, not narrowly (a margin-1 win is
worth 0.6/call, a margin-5 win 1.0/call).

Every variant below is my own original line. None copies any public
submission title, house anchor/rung text, or winner text quoted in
leaderboard-top10.md.

## The ten variants

- **V1 — Friday 4:59 deck:** "My boss sent me a 'quick task' at 4:59 on Friday, so I replied at 9:01 on Monday — with a 47-slide deck titled 'Why This Could Have Waited.'" (150 chars)
- **V2 — Exit interview / pizza:** "In my exit interview HR asked what would have kept me, so I said 'a raise' — she wrote down 'free pizza Fridays,' and that's exactly why I'm leaving." (155 chars)
- **V3 — Intern to CTO / printer:** "Kevin asked where the printer was on his first day; three years later he's the CTO, and I still ask Kevin where the printer is." (134 chars)
- **V4 — Quick question / lunch:** "My manager's 'quick question' took 45 minutes, three whiteboards, and a follow-up meeting — the question was whether I'd had lunch yet." (142 chars)
- **V5 — Unlimited PTO / Greg's desk:** "We have unlimited PTO, so I took three weeks off and came back to 412 unread emails, a new manager, and a desk HR had already given to someone named Greg." (159 chars)
- **V6 — Cybersecurity training / prince:** "I finished the cybersecurity training in 11 minutes, clicked 'I understand' 47 times, and immediately emailed my password to a prince — the course was right about me." (166 chars)
- **V7 — Birthday cake / $400 singing:** "It's my birthday, so the team bought a cake, sang, and billed the 45 minutes to my project — the cake cost $12, the singing cost $400." (139 chars)
- **V8 — IT ticket / IT is slow:** "I raised an IT ticket saying my laptop was slow; IT closed it as 'user error,' so I raised a second ticket saying IT was slow — that one is still open." (153 chars)
- **V9 — Out-of-office / left the company:** "My out-of-office says I'm on annual leave, but my boss keeps emailing 'quick one?' — so I changed it to 'I have left the company,' and he finally stopped." (158 chars)
- **V10 — First day / Greg left 2019:** "On my first day HR gave me a 90-page handbook, a laptop from 2014, and a buddy named Greg — the handbook is fiction, the laptop is a brick, and Greg left in 2019." (165 chars)

## Pairwise results

Champion V6 vs each challenger (criterion where it first differs):

- **V6 vs V1:** V1's deck is funny but the reveal is a retaliation fantasy that
  needs a beat to land; V6's self-own ("emailed my password to a prince")
  surprises instantly and the kicker ("the course was right about me")
  is a second turn. V6 wins, margin ~3.
- **V6 vs V2:** V2 is the strongest challenger — double reveal (pizza
  Fridays / that's why I'm leaving). But "HR offers pizza instead of a
  raise" is a familiar trope shape; V6's prince twist is fresher and the
  surprise is bigger. V6 wins, margin ~2.
- **V6 vs V3:** V3 is charming but its punch is a gentle inversion; V6's
  punch is a genuine laugh. V6 wins, margin ~3.
- **V6 vs V4:** V4's escalation is good, but "whether I'd had lunch yet"
  is a mild absurdity next to V6's self-incrimination. V6 wins, margin ~3.
- **V6 vs V5:** V5's escalation (412 emails, new manager, Greg's desk) is
  strong, but its punch is diffuse across three items; V6 lands one sharp
  blow. V6 wins, margin ~2.
- **V6 vs V7:** V7 is witty arithmetic ($12 vs $400) — an observation more
  than a laugh. V6 wins, margin ~3.
- **V6 vs V8:** V8's "that one is still open" is a decent twist, but the
  ticket-closure setup is inside-baseball; V6 is instantly clear. V6 wins,
  margin ~3.
- **V6 vs V9:** V9's escalation is good, but "he finally stopped" lands
  flat next to V6's double turn. V6 wins, margin ~2.
- **V6 vs V10:** V10's three-beat escalation is charming, but it is long
  and its final beat (Greg left in 2019) is weaker than V6's. V6 wins,
  margin ~2.

Runner-up semi: **V2 vs V5** — V2 wins, margin ~2 (tighter, double reveal
vs diffuse three-item punch).

## Champion

**V6** — "I finished the cybersecurity training in 11 minutes, clicked
'I understand' 47 times, and immediately emailed my password to a
prince — the course was right about me."

- Structure: 2-beat + reveal in the final clause (setup: breezed through
  training, 47 clicks; reveal: emailed password to a prince; kicker: the
  course was right about me). Twist test: passed — two turns, not one.
- Specific numbers: 11 minutes, 47 times. Named character: none needed —
  "a prince" is the specific vivid detail (Nigerian-prince scam, instantly
  understood).
- Fresh angle: no cybersecurity/training entry among the 82 public
  submission titles at build time.
- Gate: 166 chars, 168 bytes, single line, UTF-8, non-whitespace.
